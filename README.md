# Fine-Tuning-Aware Training-Example Attribution for Diagnosing Span-Based Text Predictions
Fine-tuned neural models for text prediction achieve strong performance, yet it remains difficult to identify which training examples are associated with individual predictions and whether their learned signals support or mislead the model. Existing influential-example methods often overlook multi-token span structure, rely on hypothetical parameter perturbations, or retrieve examples from representation spaces that entangle pretrained knowledge with task-specific adaptation. We propose a post-hoc framework for diagnosing span-based predictions through influential training examples. The framework isolates fine-tuning-induced activation changes, constructs span representations that preserve boundary and decision-relevant content information, and retrieves training spans whose learned adaptations are closely associated with a test prediction. It then applies activation-space signal neutralization to characterize each retrieved example as supportive or misleading under the trained model’s current parameters, without retraining, gradient access, or Hessian-based influence approximation. On biomedical semantic role labeling, the proposed method achieves an 83.3% faithfulness index and 76.6% prediction–polarity agreement, outperforming span-adapted representation- and gradient-based attribution baselines. Ablation and sensitivity analyses support the contributions of its representation, span construction, and retrieval design, while a cross-task evaluation on named entity recognition demonstrates applicability to another span-based prediction task. Finally, targeted refinement guided by misleading training examples corrects 19.1% of previously incorrect predictions at the best-performing intervention budget, showing practical value for prediction diagnosis, model debugging, and targeted training-data refinement.
