# ATaVEE: Fine-Tuning-Aware Training-Example Attribution for Diagnosing Span-Based Predictions in Text Mining
Fine-tuned models for textual data mining learn task-specific prediction behavior, yet it remains difficult to determine which training examples are associated with individual predictions and whether their learned signals support or mislead the model. Existing influential-example methods often overlook multi-token span structure, rely on hypothetical parameter perturbations, or retrieve examples from representation spaces that mix pretrained knowledge with task-specific learning. We propose ATaVEE (Activation-Task-Vector Example-based Explanation), a post-hoc method for diagnosing span-based predictions through influential training examples. ATaVEE introduces a fine-tuning-aware representation space that isolates task-specific activation changes induced by fine-tuning, constructs span representations preserving boundary and decision-relevant content information, and retrieves training spans whose learned adaptations are closely associated with a test prediction. A training-signal neutralization procedure then evaluates each retrieved example under the trained model’s current parameter state, distinguishing supportive from misleading examples without retraining or parameter-level perturbation. Experiments on biomedical semantic role labeling show that ATaVEE retrieves more behaviorally influential training spans than random same-label references and span-adapted attribution baselines, while influence direction aligns with prediction correctness. Ablation and sensitivity analyses confirm the contribution of fine-tuning-aware representation, span construction, and similarity modeling. A cross-task evaluation on named entity recognition shows that the same method extends to another span-based prediction problem. Finally, targeted refinement using misleading training examples corrects more than 19% of previously incorrect predictions, demonstrating that task-specific learned adaptations can provide actionable evidence for explaining learned predictive knowledge, diagnosing training-data effects, and refining span-based models.
